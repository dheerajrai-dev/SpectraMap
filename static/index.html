How SpectraMap Works

1. The Physical Cellular Problem

Cellular networks must share a scarce slice of radio spectrum. If two nearby towers transmit on the exact same frequency, their radio signals collide, causing dropped calls and data corruption.

2. The Discrete Mathematical Model

Vertex (Node) A cell tower transceiver with geographic coordinates (lati, loni) and coverage radius ri.
Interference Edge An undirected link between two towers whose transmission ranges overlap: d(u, v) ≤ ru + rv.
Color (Channel) An abstract frequency group (f1, f2, …). Connected nodes cannot share the same color.
Validation An independent checker verifying zero adjacent conflicts: ∀ (u, v) ∈ E, c(u) ≠ c(v).

3. Welsh-Powell vs DSATUR

Both are greedy graph coloring heuristics trying to approximate the chromatic number χ(G) with zero conflicts:

  • Welsh-Powell: Statically sorts vertices by descending degree (highest connectivity first) and assigns colors greedily.
  • DSATUR (Degree of Saturation): Dynamically chooses the uncolored vertex with the highest number of distinct colors already assigned to its neighbors, breaking ties with vertex degree. Often uses fewer colors on real-world cluster topologies!
